Riemann (domain partition)

Domain partitioned into intervals Δxi:

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx \approx \sum_{i=1}^N f(x_i^*)\Delta x_i$$

Lebesgue (range partition)

Range partitioned into intervals [yj, yj+1):

$$\int f\,d\mu \approx \sum_{j=1}^M y_j \cdot \mu(f^{-1}([y_j,y_{j+1})))$$

Convergence note: Smooth functions — Lebesgue (range midpoint) is O(1/N); Riemann (domain midpoint) is O(1/N²). Both converge to the same value (equivalence theorem). The chart shows quadrature-order, not theoretical validity.

1. Liouville & Φ(z)

By Liouville's theorem, if $e^{-x^2/2}$ had an elementary antiderivative it would be $R(x)e^{-x^2/2}$ with $R$ rational; differentiation yields a contradiction. Hence $\Phi(z)$ has no elementary closed form. We adopt the special function $\mathrm{erf}$ and evaluate it numerically.

2. IDS Anomaly Guards

Intrusion Detection Systems compute Z-scores and p-values from $\Phi(Z)$ millions of times per second. Heavy quadrature is too slow; rational approximations (Abramowitz & Stegun, derived from Taylor) enable wire-speed classification.

3. Pathological Functions

Riemann sums on Dirichlet/Thomae diverge because the upper and lower Darboux sums do not squeeze. Lebesgue integration bypasses this by measuring preimages globally: since rationals have measure 0, the integral is exactly 0 — Riemann's definition is simply not applicable.

4. Traffic Area (DDoS)

Riemann approach = uniform bitrate polling (SNMP). Lebesgue approach = state-based: total time spent at each congestion level. Both converge; Lebesgue binning is more robust to bursty traffic.
