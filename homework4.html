Theoretical Analysis & Contextual Interpretation

1. The Stochastic Model

The penetration process is modeled as a random walk, where the increment $X_i$ represents the outcome of an attack on the $i$-th system. The simulation implements two random variable models:

  • Bernoulli Model: $X_i \in \{0, 1\}$ with $P(X_i=1)=p$. This models a binary success/failure rate where the expected value is $\mu = p$ and variance is $\sigma^2 = p(1-p)$.
  • Rademacher Model: $X_i \in \{-1, +1\}$ with $p=0.5$. This models a symmetric zero-sum scenario (e.g., gain or lose a foothold) where $\mu = 0$ and $\sigma^2 = 1$.

2. The Law of Large Numbers (LLN)

The trajectory chart explicitly demonstrates the Weak Law of Large Numbers. Let $S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$. The LLN states that the sample average converges in probability to the expected value:

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} P\left(\left| \frac{S_n}{n} - \mu \right| > \epsilon\right) = 0$$

Visually, this is represented by the "funnel effect". As the number of systems $n$ increases, the random fluctuations of individual attackers cancel out, and all trajectories tightly hug the red theoretical expected value line ($\mathbb{E}[X]$).

3. Variance Scaling and Normalization

The three histograms highlight the crucial role of scaling factors in probability theory. By analyzing the theoretical variance formulas, we understand why only one normalization leads to a stable distribution:

  • $S_n$ (Total Score): $\text{Var}(S_n) = n\sigma^2$. The variance grows linearly with $n$. As seen in the first histogram, the distribution spreads out infinitely, making absolute bounds impossible to define for large $n$.
  • $S_n/n$ (Average Score): $\text{Var}(S_n/n) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}$. The variance shrinks towards zero. The middle histogram collapses into a single deterministic spike (a Dirac delta), proving the LLN.
  • $S_n/\sqrt{n}$ (Normalized): $\text{Var}(S_n/\sqrt{n}) = \sigma^2$. This is the "Goldilocks" scaling. The variance remains completely constant, preserving the shape of the distribution regardless of how large $n$ becomes.

4. Central Limit Theorem & Cyber Context

By keeping the variance constant, the $S_n/\sqrt{n}$ scaling exposes the Central Limit Theorem (CLT). For the Rademacher case, the normalized sum converges in distribution to the Standard Normal $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, which perfectly matches the overlaid red PDF curve in the final chart.

Contextual Interpretation: In a cybersecurity scenario, $n$ represents the scale of the target infrastructure. A massive $n$ means attackers' overall success rate ($S_n/n$) becomes perfectly predictable, eliminating the "luck" factor. The variable $m$ represents the population of attackers; increasing $m$ provides a richer statistical sample, smoothing out empirical distributions without altering the underlying mathematical limits.
