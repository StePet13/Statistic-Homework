Exercise 1 — Mathematical Derivation of Knuth's & Welford's Recurrence Formulas Analytical Derivation & 3-Line Algorithm

1. Knuth's Recurrence for the Mean ($\bar{x}_k$)

Given $k$ observations $x_1, \dots, x_k$, separate the $k$-th score $x_k$ from the previous $k-1$ scores:

$$\bar{x}_k = \frac{1}{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1} x_i + x_k\right) = \frac{(k-1)\bar{x}_{k-1} + x_k}{k}$$

Rearranging terms yields Knuth's recurrence formula:

$$\boxed{\bar{x}_k = \bar{x}_{k-1} + \frac{x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}}{k}}$$

From this update, we obtain two fundamental algebraic identities linking the pre-update residual $\delta_k = x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}$ and the post-update residual $\delta'_k = x_k - \bar{x}_k$:

$$\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1} = \frac{x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}}{k}, \qquad x_k - \bar{x}_k = \frac{k-1}{k}(x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})$$

2. Welford's Recurrence for Sum of Squares ($SS_k$) & Variance

Define the sum of squared deviations $SS_k = \sum_{i=1}^k (x_i - \bar{x}_k)^2$. Isolate the $k$-th term and add/subtract $\bar{x}_{k-1}$ inside the remaining sum:

$$SS_k = \sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left[(x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1}) - (\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})\right]^2 + (x_k - \bar{x}_k)^2$$

Since $\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1}) = 0$, the cross-term vanishes:

$$SS_k = SS_{k-1} + (k-1)(\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2 + (x_k - \bar{x}_k)^2$$

Substituting the two residual identities from Step 1 simplifies the increment to the product of the pre-update and post-update residuals:

$$(k-1)\frac{(x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2}{k^2} + \frac{(k-1)^2}{k^2}(x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2 = \frac{k-1}{k}(x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2 = (x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})(x_k - \bar{x}_k)$$ $$\boxed{SS_k = SS_{k-1} + (x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})(x_k - \bar{x}_k)} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \boxed{s_k^2 = \frac{SS_k}{k-1}}$$

Complete JavaScript Implementation (3-Line Core Logic)

function welfordUpdate(scores) {
  let mean = 0.0, ss = 0.0;
  for (let k = 1; k <= scores.length; k++) {
    const x = scores[k - 1];
    // --- CORE LOGIC (3 LINES) ---
    const delta = x - mean;        // 1. Pre-update residual: (x_k - mean_{k-1})
    mean += delta / k;             // 2. Knuth mean update:   mean_k = mean_{k-1} + delta / k
    ss += delta * (x - mean);      // 3. Welford SS update:   SS_k = SS_{k-1} + delta * (x_k - mean_k)
  }
  return { mean: mean, ss: ss, sampleVar: ss / (scores.length - 1), popVar: ss / scores.length };
}

Theoretical Discussion & Inferential Statistics Interpretation Academic Report

1. Why Welford's Algorithm Outperforms Naive Variance Formulas

In computational statistics, computing the sample variance via the textbook one-pass expansion $s^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 - n\bar{x}^2\right)$ is a classic anti-pattern. When the variance $\sigma^2$ is small relative to the square of the mean $\mu^2$, the terms $\sum x_i^2$ and $n\bar{x}^2$ become two huge, nearly identical floating-point numbers. Subtracting them eliminates the most significant bits—a phenomenon known as catastrophic cancellation—which can destroy precision or even yield negative variances.

By maintaining the running mean $\bar{x}_k$ (via Knuth's update) and accumulating the product of centered residuals $(x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})(x_k - \bar{x}_k)$ into $SS_k$, Welford's algorithm keeps all intermediate terms on the scale of $\sigma$ rather than $\mu$. Furthermore, it operates in a single streaming pass with $O(1)$ memory complexity, making it ideal for real-time telemetry and cybersecurity monitoring where $n$ grows continuously.

2. Unbiasedness of $\bar{X}$ and $S^2$ & The Role of Bessel's Correction

Our simulation across $m$ hackers empirically confirms two foundational theorems of sampling theory: $$\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}] = \mu \qquad \text{and} \qquad \mathbb{E}[S^2] = \sigma^2$$ By linearity of expectation, $\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}] = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}[X_i] = \mu$. For the variance, however, measuring squared deviations from the sample mean $\bar{X}$ rather than the true mean $\mu$ systematically makes the sum of squares smaller, because $\bar{X}$ is the exact value that minimizes $\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - c)^2$.

Algebraically, expanding $\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \mu)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n \left[(X_i - \bar{X}) + (\bar{X} - \mu)\right]^2$ yields: $$\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \bar{X})^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \mu)^2 - n(\bar{X} - \mu)^2$$ Taking expectations on both sides gives $\mathbb{E}[SS_n] = n\sigma^2 - n\,\text{Var}(\bar{X}) = n\sigma^2 - n\left(\frac{\sigma^2}{n}\right) = (n-1)\sigma^2$. Dividing by $n-1$ (Bessel's correction) compensates for the single degree of freedom lost by estimating $\mu$ with $\bar{X}$.

3. Why These Results Are Fundamental in Inferential Statistics

In practical applications—such as assessing network security or auditing penetration resistance—we never observe the entire population of all possible attack trajectories, nor do we know the true analytical parameters $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$ a priori. Instead, a security analyst observes one finite sample of $n$ penetration attempts.

  • Point Estimation without Systematic Bias: Unbiasedness guarantees that using $\bar{X}$ to estimate expected damage and $S^2$ to estimate volatility does not systematically under- or over-state risk across repeated audits.
  • Confidence Intervals & Hypothesis Testing: Because $\bar{X} \stackrel{\text{approx}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2/n)$ by the Central Limit Theorem and $S^2$ is a consistent estimator of $\sigma^2$, replacing the unknown $\sigma$ with $S$ allows us to construct Student-$t$ confidence intervals $\bar{X} \pm t_{\alpha/2, n-1}\frac{S}{\sqrt{n}}$ and perform rigorous statistical hypothesis tests.

4. Effect of Increasing $n$: Estimator Variance Decay & Consistency

Repeating the experiment for increasing values of $n$ demonstrates how sample size governs estimation reliability:

  • Variance of the Sample Mean: $\text{Var}(\bar{X}) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}$. Doubling the precision (halving the standard error $\sigma/\sqrt{n}$) requires quadrupling the number of tested systems $n$.
  • Variance of the Sample Variance: For i.i.d. observations with fourth central moment $\mu_4 = \mathbb{E}[(X-\mu)^4]$, the exact variance of $S^2$ is: $$\text{Var}(S^2) = \frac{1}{n}\left(\mu_4 - \frac{n-3}{n-1}\sigma^4\right)$$ As $n \to \infty$, both $\text{Var}(\bar{X}) \to 0$ and $\text{Var}(S^2) \to 0$ at rate $O(1/n)$.
  • Consistency (Convergence in Probability): By Chebyshev's inequality, any unbiased estimator whose variance shrinks to zero as $n \to \infty$ converges in probability to the true parameter ($\bar{X} \xrightarrow{P} \mu$ and $S^2 \xrightarrow{P} \sigma^2$). In the context of $m$ hackers and $n$ systems, when $n$ is small, individual hackers' sample means and variances fluctuate wildly due to luck; when $n$ is large, every single hacker's trajectory accurately reveals the true underlying vulnerability $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$.
